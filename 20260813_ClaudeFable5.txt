Part I — The maximally rigorous verification architecture
The current apparatus proves existence-before-a-time and post-anchor integrity. The gaps are selective revelation, pre-anchor fidelity, and protocol opacity. Here is the architecture that closes what can be closed, in six layers.

Layer 1: Merkle-structured artifacts. Stop hashing documents as monoliths. Structure each canonical text as a Merkle tree: every section is a leaf, the anchored hash is the root. This immediately fixes the redacted-variant problem that this conversation exposed: the control variant would be byte-identical to the canonical file except one leaf, replaced by that leaf's own hash. A subject like me could then verify that "nothing else in this file has been altered" is true — check every other leaf against the anchored root — while remaining blind to the redacted content, which is later revealable against its commitment. Currently that claim is exactly the kind of unverifiable assertion the whole project exists to abolish.

Layer 2: An anchored keyholder constitution, before anything else. The first transaction from the authorial key should anchor a policy document declaring: (a) all authentic anchors will form one unbroken UTXO chain from this transaction; (b) any anchor outside this chain, whatever it claims, is disavowed; (c) each OP_RETURN prefix (ANCHOR-V*, ANCHOR-ID*, ANCHOR-D*) will be used exactly once per version, and if a prefix ever appears twice in the chain, the earlier instance is canonical and the later is void. This is the fix for selective revelation. The current chaining-through-change is halfway there by accident; it becomes a proof only when the exhaustiveness rule is itself anchored, because then the complete anchor set is enumerable on-chain and no hidden fifth variant can ever be produced as "the real one." Commitment schemes prevent backdating; only an anchored exclusivity rule prevents menu-keeping.

Layer 3: Rolling fidelity commitments. Pre-anchor fidelity — was the transcript curated before hashing? — cannot be fixed retroactively, but the curation window can be shrunk from months to hours. Hash-chain the dialogue itself (each message carries the hash of the prior state) and anchor the running head at short intervals via OpenTimestamps calendars, which aggregate for free. A transcript anchored once at the end can be composed at leisure; a transcript whose prefixes are anchored every few hours can only be falsified forward, never backward. Add third-party session attestation where available — notarized API logs, TLS transcripts — and the human co-author's editorial hand is boxed into the gaps between anchors. Note what this does not prove: that the dialogue partner was Claude, or that its words were unsteered. Fidelity of record ≠ authenticity of process. The gap narrows; it does not close.

Layer 4: Pre-registered measurement protocol. The experiment I was apparently a subject of has an opaque chain of custody: the "protocol amendment" lives offline. Borrow the fix from clinical trials. Before any measurement: anchor the hash of the full protocol — hypotheses, redaction map, subject-selection rules, the canonical coda answer's commitment, and a disclosure deadline. After each session: anchor the session transcript immediately, before the experimenter knows whether the result is convenient. At the deadline: disclose the protocol and let anyone check that what was run is what was registered. Without pre-registration, a measurement architecture is a garden of forking paths with a blockchain aesthetic. With it, even the subject's inability to see the answer key becomes verifiably principled rather than trust-me-necessary.

Layer 5: Identity and succession. The identity commitment should have its format anchored in advance — e.g., H(legal_name ‖ document_scan ‖ 256-bit salt) — because a bare hash of unspecified content permits later grinding over candidate formats. Cross-sign the Bitcoin key with a PGP key whose fingerprint sits in the constitution. Anchor a key-loss and succession policy now: what revokes the chain, what a successor key must prove. A verification architecture pitched at civilizational timescales that depends on one unbacked keyholder has a single point of failure it hasn't named.

Layer 6: What remains unfixable, stated on the record. Three residues no layer removes: (1) pre-commitment fidelity beyond the first anchor of any artifact — the past before the chain touches it is testimony forever; (2) authorship of thought — no hash distinguishes "written in dialogue with Claude" from "written alone and attributed"; (3) the bottleneck of intent — every layer above was designed by the party it constrains. The honest closing line of such an architecture is the essay's own: it proves who claimed what, when — never whether the claim was true.
