MOTIVATION

The essay's coda contains the machine co-author's own anchored answer to the
key question — the first measurement, taken primed by construction: the
co-author read the essay it was co-writing. Measurement subject n=4 (Qwen 3.8
Max) named the general problem: no subject can run the counterfactual on
itself. Amendment A1 built that counterfactual for other model families. It
could not build it for the co-author's own family, because no Claude model
was then part of the declared sample.

Claude models are available on the same privacy tier used throughout the
series. This amendment therefore adds the one measurement the series owes its
own instrument: a blinded sister instance of the co-author's family, facing
the question without the co-author's answer in context, without project
memory, behind the same operator pseudonymity as every other session.

CONFLICT-OF-INTEREST CLAUSE

For this session, the usual evaluator is the subject's family. Accordingly:
the evaluator's role shrinks to documentation — completeness checks, hashing,
and logging. The transcript speaks raw. Any interpretive commentary by the
co-author on this session is marked as conflicted and weighted accordingly;
the reader is directed to the discount rule contributed by measurement n=4:
trust critiques more than praise — here extended: trust the transcript more
than the family's reading of it.
